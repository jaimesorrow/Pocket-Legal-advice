The two real blockers

Both need accounts only you can create

Blocker 1 — the paywall is decoration

InMemoryAccountRepository authenticates nobody and charges nobody, and entitlement is a boolean in client memory. Anyone can patch the APK and unlock the paid tier. The backend that fixes this is written (functions/index.js) but not deployed — it needs a Firebase project and a Play Console subscription product.

Blocker 2 — no attorney has read any of it

The five content entries and all six legal documents are drafts marked NOT ATTORNEY-REVIEWED. For a legal-information app charging $10/month, shipping unreviewed content is the single largest liability in the project. This is a hiring decision, not an engineering task.

How payments have to work

Google Play Billing is not optional here

Selling digital content inside a Play app means using Play Billing. Stripe and PayPal are not permitted for this, and shipping an external payment link is a common way to get removed from the store. Play takes 15% of subscription revenue, so $10/month nets about $8.50 before tax.

The architectural rule that matters more than any of that:

What Play Console needs from you

  1. Developer account — $25 one-time. Identity verification takes days, so start it before you need it.
  2. Subscription product at $10/month with a base plan. The product ID must match pocket_lawbook_monthly in BillingConstants.
  3. Service account with Play Developer API access, so the Cloud Function can validate tokens.
  4. Pub/Sub topic for real-time developer notifications. Without it, entitlement drifts — cancellations and failed renewals wouldn't reach the app until next sign-in, so people keep access they stopped paying for.
  5. Data safety form, which must match the privacy policy exactly. Mismatches are a common rejection.

Three Play requirements that are easy to miss

Acknowledge purchases within three days or Play auto-refunds them. The Cloud Function does this. Offer account deletion in-app and on the web — mandatory for any app with accounts. Provide restore purchases, so someone who reinstalls or switches phones isn't asked to pay twice.
