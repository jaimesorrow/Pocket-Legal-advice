The pipeline behind the app is real and tested — 21 passing tests covering the repository, view model, and state machine. What is missing is everything the user would touch. There is no navigation-compose, no Room, no HTTP client, and no DI. So the navigation flow below is a greenfield decision, not a refactor.

On not having accounts

Keep it that way deliberately. No account means no server-side record of who asked what about their arrest, their eviction, or their custody case. In this category that is a genuine safety property, not just a shorter funnel — and it is much harder to add privacy back later than to keep it now.

The twelve screens

Grouped by build order, because that is the decision the grouping encodes

Core

5 screens · ship this first

Everything here earns its place by answering a question a scared person has right now. If a screen is not on this list, it can wait.

  1. Homeroute: homestart destination

    Cold launch lands here. Two entry points, not one: Ask a question opens the chat, Describe what happened opens the analysis pipeline you already built. Plus a persistent Emergency button.

  2. Answerroute: answer/{threadId}AlaskaFederal

    The chat thread. Every answer carries a jurisdiction chip so the reader always knows whether they are reading Alaska law or federal law. Architecture is below.

  3. Sourceroute: source/{citation}offline

    The payoff for "credited sources": full verbatim statute text, its citation, and its effective date. Every citation anywhere in the app deep-links here.

  4. Situation analysisroute: analyze

    Your existing pipeline, given a face at last. Describe a situation, get back verified violations. Already fully specified by the test suite.

  5. Action stepsroute: steps/{violationKey}offline

    One VerifiedActionStep, rendered. Strings pass through byte-for-byte from the database, exactly as the tests demand.

Supporting

4 screens · second release

These make the app worth keeping installed between emergencies.

  1. Browseroute: browse

    Topic library for people who do not know the words to search for. Alaska needs its own topics, not a generic set: subsistence hunting and fishing, ANCSA and Alaska Native rights, VPSO encounters in villages, cold-weather habitability and heat obligations, commercial fishing and maritime work.

  2. Savedroute: savedoffline

    Local bookmarks. On-device only — no sync, because sync implies an account.

  3. Emergency cardroute: emergencyoffline

    One tap from Home. A read-aloud script for a stop or arrest, sized for a locked screen at arm's length. Must work at airplane mode with a dead network.

  4. Find helproute: help

    The exit ramp to a human: Alaska Legal Services Corporation, the Alaska Bar Association referral line, the public defender agency. This screen is what makes "not legal advice" honest rather than a disclaimer.

System

3 screens · required, not optional

Two of these are compliance surface. Build them early even though nobody demos them.

  1. First-run disclaimerroute: welcomeonce

    The only thing between launch and Home, shown exactly once. One acknowledgment that this is legal information covering Alaska and federal law, not legal advice. This is not a signup — no field to fill in, no identity captured.

  2. Settingsroute: settings

    Text size, language, clear local history.

  3. About & sourcesroute: about

    Content provenance, who reviewed it, when each source was last verified, and licensing. In a legal app this page is the credibility argument.

How navigation flows

One Activity, one nav graph, three tabs

The chat, without breaking your guarantee

Hybrid: the model may frame, it may never claim

Your test suite asserts that no raw API text ever reaches the UI — including that answer strings are not even a substring of any model-authored field. A chat feature collides with that head-on. The hybrid design resolves it by splitting the model's job in two: it may write exactly one plain-language framing sentence, and it may select which vetted passages answer the question. It never writes a legal claim, and it never writes a citation.

Two consequences worth accepting up front. The corpus becomes the product — the app can only answer what you have vetted, so coverage is a content problem, not an engineering one. And because retrieval and rendering are on-device, the app still answers with the network off; only the framing sentence disappears. That is the right failure mode for a rights app.

Test to add before the chat ships

Extend the existing zero-hallucination suite: assert that a rendered answer's citation string always matches a record in the corpus, and that the framing sentence is rejected if it contains a digit-and-section pattern. That turns your architectural rule into something CI enforces, the same way the current 21 tests do.

What else belongs in an app like this

Ordered by what will hurt most if it is missing

Offline everything

before launch

The moment this app matters most — roadside, detained, a phone about to be taken, a village with no bars — is the moment the network is gone. Ship the corpus in the APK and treat any network dependency as an enhancement that degrades silently.

Last-verified dates and a review workflow

before launch

Statutes change. Every entry needs an effective date, a last-reviewed date, and the initials of the licensed Alaska attorney who reviewed it. Content review is the recurring operational cost of this product; design the pipeline for it now rather than discovering it later.

Jurisdiction on every surface

before launch

Tag every record ALASKA or FEDERAL at the schema level, not in the copy, and render the chip everywhere an answer appears. "Including all federal laws" under the app name sets the expectation; the chips are what keep it honest answer by answer.

Plain language, seriously measured

high

Statutory language is unreadable under stress. Target roughly a 6th-grade reading level for the vetted summaries and measure it in the content pipeline. This does more for real-world usefulness than any feature on this page.

Languages Alaska actually speaks

high

Spanish and Tagalog have substantial speaker populations in Anchorage, and Iñupiaq, Yup'ik and Gwich'in matter in communities where the nearest attorney is a flight away. Localization here is a reach decision, not a polish decision.

Recording rights, handled carefully

high

Alaska is a one-party-consent state, which makes an in-app recorder genuinely useful during an encounter. It also makes getting the details wrong genuinely harmful. If you build it, have it reviewed as legal content, not as a feature.

Privacy posture, stated plainly

high

No account is the foundation. Finish it: keep query history on-device, keep analytics to screen-level counters with no query text, and say so in the app rather than only in a policy.

Accessibility

medium

Large text, high contrast, full TalkBack labelling, and a one-handed reachable Emergency button. Assume a cracked screen, gloves, and cold hands — this is Alaska.

An honest "we can't answer that"

medium

Your NoVerifiedData state already anticipates this. Give it a real screen that routes to Find help rather than a dead end. Knowing when to send someone to a human is the feature that keeps the app on the right side of the advice line.

Suggested next step

Add navigation-compose and Room, then build the vertical slice that already has a passing spec: Home → Situation analysis → Action steps. It exercises the whole pipeline your 21 tests cover, needs no model and no network, and gets a real screen in front of the code you have already proven. The chat can land on top of a working corpus afterwards.
